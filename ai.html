Five Connections

01

Systems Thinking → Cybersecurity

Systems thinking and cybersecurity both involve understanding how components interact, identifying weaknesses, and considering how failures affect the whole. These connections support thoughtful decisions about protecting systems and information.

02

Risk → Software Development

Risk thinking can also apply to software development. Questions about what could fail, how inputs should be handled, where weaknesses may exist, and how systems can be made more reliable are relevant to both risk management and software design.

03

Running → Persistence

Running and technical learning both reward consistency. Progress often comes through repeated effort, practice, and gradual improvement rather than immediate results.

04

Reading → Perspective

An interest in psychology, philosophy, and human behavior can introduce different ways of thinking about decisions, people, systems, and technology. These perspectives can complement more technical forms of problem-solving.

05

Curiosity → Building

Curiosity becomes practical when it leads to experimentation. Exploring coding, cybersecurity, and software development can turn questions into tangible projects that can be tested, revised, and improved.
